# Proposed page: What Your Child Will Learn

**Suggested URL:** `/what-your-child-will-learn`  
**Navigation label:** What they&apos;ll learn  
**Primary audience:** Parents deciding whether Blooming Tastebuds is worthwhile for their child.  
**Primary action:** Find a class

---

## The job of this page

This is not a booking page and it is not a recipe catalogue. It gives parents confidence that Blooming Tastebuds offers a considered learning experience: practical kitchen skills, confidence, healthier food awareness, and a sense of independence.

The page should answer these questions before a parent has to ask them:

- What will my child actually learn?
- Is it appropriate for their age and confidence level?
- Will they be safe and supported?
- What kinds of food will they make?
- What difference might this make outside the class?

## 3. Five things they&apos;ll take away

Use five illustrated cards in a staggered grid. These are outcomes, not lesson titles.

### Kitchen confidence

**Making food from scratch helps children see that the kitchen is a place for them.**  
They practise each step with support, then build the confidence to have a go at home.

### Safe, practical skills

**From washing hands to using age-appropriate tools, good habits become second nature.**  
They learn to measure, mix, chop, prepare and cook safely.

### Healthier food awareness

**Children explore colourful ingredients without pressure.**  
They begin to understand balance, texture, flavour and how everyday food can help them feel good.

### Creativity and problem-solving

**There is more than one way to make something delicious.**  
They choose toppings, adapt flavours and learn that mistakes can be part of making.

### Independence and teamwork

**Cooking is a life skill—and a shared experience.**  
They learn to organise their space, work with others and feel proud of contributing.

### Visual direction

Use the site&apos;s existing warm green, terracotta and soft cream palette. Each card should have a simple illustrative icon and one candid supporting image across the whole section—do not make it look like a corporate feature grid.

## 4. Two cooking journeys

### Heading

The right challenge at every stage.

### Junior Cooks — ages 5–11

**A joyful start to cooking from scratch.**

Junior Cooks explore fresh ingredients, learn everyday kitchen routines and discover that healthy food can be colourful, creative and delicious. We introduce skills step by step, with plenty of encouragement and room to try.

**They may practise**

- washing, measuring and mixing;
- safe cutting with age-appropriate guidance;
- recognising vegetables, herbs and flavours;
- making choices and presenting a dish proudly.

**Example dishes**  
Rainbow wraps · Tortilla pizzas · Fresh pasta · Vegetable muffins

### Teen Chefs — ages 12–18

**Real-world skills for growing independence.**

Teen Chefs move beyond following instructions. They build technique, understand how to plan a balanced meal and gain confidence making food for themselves, friends and family.

**They may practise**

- knife technique and confident food preparation;
- balancing flavours and adapting recipes;
- planning a meal and managing a budget;
- cooking independently and presenting food with pride.

**Example dishes**  
Homemade curries · Pasta sauces · Nourishing lunches · Baking projects

### Design treatment

Two large editorial cards rather than small equal columns. Each needs a real, age-appropriate image and a clear link: **See available Junior Cook classes** / **See available Teen Chef classes**.

## 5. Inside a typical term

### Heading

How learning builds, one delicious dish at a time.

### Suggested copy

Our terms are designed to build confidence gradually. A child might begin by getting to know the kitchen and preparing simple ingredients, then use those foundations to create fuller dishes, make choices of their own and share what they have made.

### Example term pathway

| Stage | What children explore | The learning behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Start with confidence | Kitchen routines, hygiene and simple preparation | Feeling comfortable, safe and included |
| Discover ingredients | Colour, texture, vegetables, herbs and flavours | Curiosity and willingness to try |
| Build technique | Measuring, mixing, cutting and cooking methods | Practical coordination and safe habits |
| Make it their own | Toppings, seasoning, plating and small adaptations | Creativity and decision-making |
| Share and celebrate | Serving, tasting and talking about the finished dish | Pride, communication and reflection |

### Important wording

This is an illustration of the learning journey, not a fixed promise of exact recipes. Specific upcoming term menus should remain available from the relevant class listing.

## 7. How we teach

### Heading

Supportive teaching, hands-on from the start.

### Suggested copy

Children learn best by doing. Every session is led in a calm, encouraging environment where children can ask questions, practise new skills and take pride in the food they make.

**Every session includes**

- a clear introduction to the day&apos;s dish and skills;
- guided, hands-on preparation;
- age-appropriate safety and hygiene routines;
- time to create, taste and celebrate the finished food.

### Visual direction

Use a four-step horizontal story with candid class images, not four generic icons.

## 8. What parents notice at home

### Heading

The learning doesn&apos;t stop at the classroom door.

### Suggested copy

Parents often tell us that their children become more curious about ingredients, more willing to help prepare a meal and more confident saying, “I can make that.”

The goal is not perfection. It is helping young people feel capable, curious and proud in the kitchen.

### Supporting proof

Place one genuine parent, school or community testimonial here. The existing Ark Elvin Academy review would be a strong starting point, though a short parent quote would be even more powerful.

## Recommended MVP scope

Build this as a mostly evergreen page using existing photography and recipe data. It does **not** need new admin fields, payment changes or full recipe pages.

1. Hero and five learning outcomes.
2. Junior Cooks / Teen Chefs learning journeys.
3. A curated recipe-and-skills gallery using existing recipe records.
4. “How we teach,” reassurance and class-search CTA.
5. Navigation and contextual links from Home and Classes.

## Optional later enhancement

Create shareable, individual programme pages for specific terms. Those pages would combine this evergreen learning story with that term&apos;s exact schedule, dates, venue, availability and booking button.
